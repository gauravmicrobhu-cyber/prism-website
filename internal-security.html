Pillar One

Internal Security

Some of the most consequential decisions a state makes are never announced as decisions at all — they surface later, in the archive, as the cables and memos of officials who were simply doing their jobs. This pillar is built on the belief that primary sources, read patiently and in full, tell a truer story than any retrospective narrative built around them.

India's internal security landscape did not emerge from a single moment — it was shaped by decisions made under pressure, across decades, by people who often could not see how the story would end. Understanding it requires more than a chronology; it requires reading the actual record left behind by the people who lived through it, in their own words, before those words were smoothed into official history.

PRISM's work in this pillar starts with the Kashmir dispute — not as a single flashpoint, but as a continuous thread running from the earliest days of independence through the present, told through the declassified American diplomatic record and cross-checked wherever possible against Indian, British, and UN primary sources.

What the archive has already surfaced

Among the findings: the actual State Department policy paper, signed by Secretary of State George Marshall on the day the UN Security Council first took up Kashmir, showing the plebiscite was America's own preferred outcome from day one — not something imposed by London or the UN. A secret partition proposal Washington and India's own diplomats each considered independently, months apart, neither willing to say so publicly. A Communist-bloc UN commissioner's private counsel to Kashmir's own government against American-backed arbitration. And India's Cabinet privately preparing, in 1950, to accept losing the Kashmir Valley through a plebiscite it expected to lose — a fact that complicates the simplicity of India's later public position.

The work continues session by session, chapter by chapter, through the FRUS volumes that follow — a deliberate, transparent, and slow-built record rather than a finished conclusion.

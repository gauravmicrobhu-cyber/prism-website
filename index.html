Why three pillars, not one

Most security research treats "internal" and "external" threats as separate disciplines, and treats technology as a footnote to both. We don't. A dispute inside a nation's own borders shapes how it negotiates abroad. A rival's information campaign is inseparable from its strategic posture. And a biotechnology breakthrough with no stated military purpose can still redraw the security landscape faster than any treaty.

Each pillar is built around a primary research instrument — not a static report, but a living body of work that gets read, verified, and extended over time. We would rather show our sourcing than assert our conclusions.

About PRISM

An independent institute built around evidence, not press releases.

PRISM — the Policy Research Institute for Security, Intelligence & Modern Threats — studies security as it actually moves: across borders, across domains, and across the line between a nation's internal life and its place in the world.

Most security research treats "internal" and "external" threats as separate disciplines, and treats technology as a footnote to both. We don't. A dispute inside a nation's own borders shapes how it negotiates abroad. A rival's information campaign is inseparable from its strategic posture. And a biotechnology breakthrough with no stated military purpose can still redraw the security landscape faster than any treaty. PRISM organizes its work around three pillars — Internal Security, Geopolitics & National Security, and Technology & National Security — precisely because the boundaries between them are where most analysis goes wrong.

Each pillar is anchored by a primary research instrument, not a static report: a living body of work that gets read, verified, and extended over time. We would rather show our sourcing than assert our conclusions.

Our Approach
  • Primary sources first. Declassified cables, verified incident reports, and direct dataset access take priority over secondary commentary, wherever they exist.
  • Every claim is tagged by how it was verified. Full text read, substantial excerpt, cited but not yet read, or secondary account — the distinction is preserved, not smoothed over.
  • Silence is reported honestly. A research pull that finds nothing new is logged as such, with the reasoning stated — not quietly skipped to preserve an appearance of constant activity.
  • Gaps are disclosed, not hidden. When a data feed is stale or a claim can't be corroborated, that status is shown on the page itself, not buried in a footnote.
Who We Are

PRISM is an independently founded research effort, built to produce evidence-first instruments rather than one-off reports. The team behind it is intentionally kept small and working — research output speaks for the institute, not individual bylines.

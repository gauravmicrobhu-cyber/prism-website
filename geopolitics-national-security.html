Pillar Two

Geopolitics & National Security

A hostile information campaign leaves a trail — a deepfake, a coordinated hashtag, a fabricated quote attributed to a serving officer. The trail is often visible in real time, if you know where to look and refuse to log anything you can't actually verify.

Modern strategic competition is no longer confined to troop movements and treaty language. Narrative control — who gets believed, whose version of events reaches the public first, which fabrication survives long enough to shape opinion before it's debunked — has become a genuine instrument of state competition, waged continuously rather than only during declared crises.

PRISM's work in this pillar centers on Pakistan's information warfare campaign against India — not as an assumption, but as a claim that has to earn its place in the record through the same evidentiary discipline any other intelligence assessment would require.

The discipline behind the registry
  • Explicit attribution required. A claim without clear Pakistan-linked sourcing doesn't get logged, however plausible it looks.
  • Duplicates are checked, not assumed absent. Every candidate is cross-referenced against the existing registry before being treated as new.
  • Pending, not promoted. New candidates sit in review before entering the live registry — nothing goes live on the strength of a single automated pass.
  • Silence is reported honestly. A pull that finds nothing new is logged as such, with the reasoning stated — not quietly skipped to preserve an appearance of constant activity.

An automated research pipeline is under active development, designed to run this same evidentiary process on a scheduled cadence rather than only when manually triggered — bringing the registry closer to real time without loosening the standard that makes it worth trusting in the first place.

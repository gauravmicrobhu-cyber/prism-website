Pillar Three

Technology & National Security

Warfare's domains have expanded in a predictable sequence — land, sea, air, then nuclear, then space, then cyber, and now the information and cognitive space itself. Technology hasn't just changed how each domain is fought in; it has been the reason new domains open at all.

This pillar covers that full sequence: cyber operations and critical-infrastructure vulnerability; space as a contested military domain, from anti-satellite capability to the civil-military interface; AI and unmanned systems — UAVs foremost among them — and their effect on command and split-second decision-making; the long-run threat quantum computing poses to current encryption; and biotechnology, where a dual-use breakthrough can move from a research lab to something with real security implications without ever being classified or restricted.

PRISM's work in this pillar starts from a simple premise: technology rarely announces when it becomes a security question. Most of these shifts are visible only in retrospect, which is why they're worth tracking as they happen.

What CONTAINMENT has already surfaced — the biosecurity corner of this pillar

Among the findings: a Guardrail Matrix showing that none of the major AI biodesign models currently in wide use — AlphaFold 3, ESM3, OpenCRISPR-1, Evo 2 — require any form of identity verification before use. A Mitigation Coverage module mapping nine active defense-in-depth strategies against six distinct threat-actor profiles, following a direct assessment of RAND Corporation's own 2026 biosecurity strategy report. And a historical incidents map spanning confirmed lab accidents, insider theft, and grey-zone unregistered facilities — with a deliberate, documented decision to exclude entries that couldn't be independently corroborated, rather than pad the record with unverified claims.

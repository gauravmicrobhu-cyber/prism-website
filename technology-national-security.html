Pillar Three

Technology & National Security

Warfare's domains have expanded in a predictable sequence — land, sea, air, then nuclear, then space, then cyber, and now the information and cognitive space itself. Technology hasn't just changed how each domain is fought in; it has been the reason new domains open at all.

This pillar covers that full sequence, not one slice of it. Cyber operations and critical-infrastructure vulnerability. Space as a contested military domain in its own right, from anti-satellite capability to the civil-military interface. Artificial intelligence's effect on command, autonomous systems, and decision-making at machine speed. Quantum computing's long-run threat to current encryption. And biotechnology — where a dual-use breakthrough can move from a research lab to something with genuine security implications without ever being classified, restricted, or even intended that way.

PRISM's work in this pillar is built around a simple premise: technology rarely announces when it becomes a security question. Most of these shifts are visible only in retrospect — which is exactly why they're worth tracking as they happen, not just writing up after the fact.
